## 1. Experiment Overview

- **Fleet:** 43 stateless TrainTicket deployments scaled to `replicas=8` with default resources (requests 500m/512Mi, limits 2/1Gi)  
- **HPAs:** Deleted (`hpa_control.py suspend`) — PBScaler controller idle  
- **Schedulable fleet:** 65/646 labels ready (65 ready replicas out of 646 total across 43 deployments)  
- **Load:** k6 open-loop flat-arrival (`FLAT_RPS=1000`) through `train-ticket-gateway-istio` NodePort `10.10.1.2:30080` from loadgen only  
- **Telemetry:** In-pod collector (`collect_training_data.py`, 5s interval) → Mimir istio/kube series → `metrics.csv` per rung  
- **Cap criterion = services stop responding** (not delay): front success <0.5, interrupted k6 iterations >0, ≥12 services success<0.9 for >40 % of window, sustained iters/s < 60 % of target  
- **Warmup:** 15 min at 10 rps → 9,001 iterations, 0 interrupted

## 3. Latency Cliff Analysis

- **80→950 rps:** P90 consistently 9.7–9.9 ms (physical baseline, 20× under SLO)
- **1000 rps:** P90 median jumps to **13.6 ms** (p95 23.9 ms, max 134.9 ms) — first latency bend
- **1025–1100 rps:** P90 median 71–81 ms, p95 2.5–21 s, 27–39 SLO violations
- **Key:** Despite the latency cliff, **k6 delivery remained 100%** (0 interrupted iterations) and **front success stayed ≥95.5%**

**Equivalent MAX_REQUESTS:** The A/B run at MAX_REQUESTS=5000 peaked at ~68 rps. Extrapolating: 1000 rps ≈ **MAX_REQUESTS 73,500**; 1100 rps ≈ **81,000**.

## 7. Conclusion

The fixed-8-replica fleet on TrainTicket handles **1000 rps open-loop** with zero failures by the cap criterion. The latency cliff at ~1025 rps is a queueing knee, not a hard failure point. The practical operating ceiling is **950 rps** to stay on the flat 9.9 ms P90 floor with full margin. The 1100 rps rung demonstrates the fleet can absorb 6× the A/B offer (MAX_REQUESTS 5000 → ~81,000) before SLO violations appear — though success remains ≥95.5%.
